β-GALACTOSIDASE ASSAY

β-galactosidase activity assay

Principle

β-galactosidase activity is measured using ONPG as a substrate. The reaction produces a yellow product, which is measured spectrophotometrically at 420 nm. Protein concentration is determined separately using the Bradford assay and is used to normalize the β-galactosidase activity.

Pellet Preparation

  1. Plate strains on YES plates.
  2. Incubate O/N in 4 mL liquid YES at 30 °C.
  3. Refresh overnight cultures by inoculating to OD600 = 0.35 in 25 mL liquid YES.
  4. Grow until OD600 = 0.8–0.85.
  5. Centrifuge cells (~2650 rpm, 6 min) and discard the medium.
  6. Wash in 1 mL dH2O, discard the supernatant, and freeze the pellets at −80 °C.

Cell Lysis + β-Galactosidase Assay

  1. Resuspend cells in 250 µL breaking buffer.
  2. Add glass beads (until the liquid reaches the meniscus).
  3. Add 12.5 µL PMSF.
  4. Vortex 3 × 2 min at top speed, with chilling on ice between vortexing steps.
  5. Add 250 µL of breaking buffer and mix well.
  6. Transfer lysed cells (supernatant) to new microtubes, avoiding the transfer of glass beads.
  7. Centrifuge for 15 min at 4 °C.
  8. Transfer the supernatant to new microtubes (store at −20 °C).
  9. Determine the protein concentration using the Bradford assay.
  10. Mix 100–250 µL of cell extract with Z buffer (total volume of 1 mL).
  11. Preincubate the mixture for 5 min at 28 °C in a water bath.
  12. Add 0.2 mL of ONPG (note the time!) — reaction initiation.
  13. Wait ~1 h until the mixture gains a pale yellow colour.
  14. Stop the reaction by adding 0.5 mL of Na2CO3 (note the time!).
  15. Measure at OD420.
Calculation:
(OD420 × 1.7) / (0.0045 × 1 × protein concentration × time in min)

Bradford Assay – Protein Concentration

Standard + Sample Preparation

  1. Dilute the Bradford reagent fivefold in dH2O (1 Bradford : 4 dH2O).
    • This assay uses Coomassie Brilliant Blue.
  2. Prepare standards by serial dilution: 0; 0.125; 0.25; 0.5; 0.75; 1 mg of BSA in 1 mL of diluted Bradford dye.
    • BSA is diluted in breaking buffer (assay buffer).
  3. Prepare samples by adding 10–20 µL of the sample to 1 mL of diluted Bradford dye and mix.
  4. Incubate for a couple of minutes at RT.
  5. Measure at OD595 nm (blank – breaking buffer (assay buffer)).
    • Calculate the calibration curve.
    • Determine protein concentration.
  • Incompatible with detergents in buffer (e.g. SDS, Triton X-100, Tween).
  • Incompatible with common reducing agents (like DTT or β-mercaptoethanol).
  • Limitations concerning amino-acid composition (check).

Solution Composition

Solution Composition
Breaking buffer (100 mL) 100 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8): 10 mL;
1 mM DTT: 100 µL (stock 1 M);
20% glycerol: 40 mL + 49.9 mL dH2O.
PMSF stock (0.1 M) 17.4 mg PMSF (100 mM); 1 mL 2-isopropanol.
Toxic! Store at −20 °C.
DTT (1 M) 1.54 g in 10 mL sterile ddH2O.
Filter-sterilise using a 0.2 µm filter. Store at −20 °C (should always be fresh – not stable).
Z buffer (100 mL) 60 mM Na2HPO4 × 7H2O: 0.8 g;
40 mM NaH2PO4 × 2H2O: 0.62 g;
10 mM KCl: 500 µL (stock 2 M);
1 mM MgSO4: 50 µL (stock 2 M);
10% glycerol: 10 mL + dH2O to 100 mL;
pH 7.
Right before use, add 50 mM 2-mercaptoethanol: 270 µL.
Store at 4 °C.
ONPG 4 mg/mL in Z buffer.
Store at −20 °C.
1 M Na2CO3 10.6 g in 100 mL dH2O.
