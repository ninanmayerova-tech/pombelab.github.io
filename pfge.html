PFGE

Whole chromosomes + rDNA preparation

PRINCIPLE

PFGE (pulsed-field gel electrophoresis) is used to separate and visualize the chromosomes of fission yeast. Agarose plugs are prepared from intact cells so that genomic DNA remains protected from shearing, followed by enzymatic treatment and electrophoresis of whole chromosomes or rDNA.

CELL PREPARATION

1. Grow cells to stationary phase (on a plate for one week or in liquid medium for an appropriate time).
  • Check the phase by microscopy: cells should be small and round.
2. Grow cells to exponential phase in liquid medium.
  • Check the phase by microscopy: cells should be longer.
3. Synchronize cells into meiosis.

DAY 1

2. Prepare 1.2% low melting point agarose in EDTA (pH 8), dissolve at 80 °C, and keep at 56 °C until use.
3. Collect 1.6 × 10⁸ cells by centrifugation and remove the supernatant.
Alternatively: collect 3 × 10⁸ cells → approximately 4 good plugs (1.6 × 10⁸ cells → approximately 2 good plugs).
4. Wash the pellets once with 1 M EDTA (pH 8).
5. Resuspend the cells in 50 µL of Lysis solution 1 (freshly prepared).
  • If using 3 × 10⁸ cells → 100 µL of Lysis solution 1.
6. Add 30 µL of 1 M sorbitol + 0.1 M EDTA (pH 8).
  • If using 3 × 10⁸ cells → 30 µL of 1 M sorbitol + 0.1 M EDTA (pH 8).
7. Gently mix the suspension with an equal volume of low melting point agarose.
  • Measure the volume before adding agarose.
8. Transfer the mixture into the gel plug mold (2–5 plugs per strain).
  • Use tape to prevent leaking (single use).
9. Incubate at 4 °C for at least 15 minutes.
10. Transfer the agarose plugs into 2 mL Eppendorf tubes, ideally using a small, narrow spatula.
11. Add 1 mL of Lysis solution 2 (freshly prepared).
12. Incubate at 37 °C for at least 2 hours (preferably overnight) on a rotating wheel at 4 rpm.

DAY 2

13. Wash the agarose plugs twice with 1 mL of 50 mM EDTA (pH 8).
14. Add 1 mL of Proteinase solution (freshly prepared).
15. Incubate overnight at 37 °C on a rotating wheel at 4 rpm.

DAY 3

16. Wash the agarose plugs twice with 1 mL of 50 mM EDTA (pH 8); store at 4 °C (plugs are good for 3 months).
  • Whole chromosomes: the plugs are now ready to use; before use, equilibrate them as described in step 17.
  • For rDNA: transfer 2 plugs to new microtubes with 1 mL of 50 mM EDTA (pH 8) and continue to step 18.
17. Just before use, equilibrate agarose plugs in 1 mL 1× TE at 37 °C for 1 hour with gentle rotation.
18. Incubate each plug twice with 10× Vplug (or 500 µL): 10 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8) + 1 mM EDTA (pH 8) for 1 hour at 37 °C on a rotating wheel at 4 rpm.
  • Wash the plugs several times with Tris-HCl (pH 8).
19. Incubate with 1× restriction-enzyme reaction buffer for 1 hour at 37 °C on a rotating wheel; repeat to equilibrate twice.
  • For 8–10 plugs, use 1.8 mL of 1× buffer in a 2 mL microtube.
  • For 2 plugs, approximately 0.5 mL buffer should suffice.
20. Remove the buffer and add a new volume: 2–3 plugs in 0.4 mL of 1× buffer (minimum 0.4 mL) in a 2 mL microtube.
21. Add 200–300 U of ScaI (20 µL at 10 U/µL) per sample and incubate during the day at 37 °C with light shaking.
  • In the evening, add 100 µL of 1× buffer + 10 µL of ScaI.
  • Do not use FD enzyme!
  • Start: 13:00; end: next day at 08:00.

DAY 4

22. Equilibrate as described in step 17.

PFGE GEL AND APERTURE PREPARATION

Pulsaphor Unit (LKB)

Gel preparation

1. Keep the remaining LMPA gel at 48 °C.
2. Prepare a 0.8% agarose gel — 150 mL 0.5× TBE (1.2 g of the agarose designated for PFGE) and allow it to cool (if it is warmer, it will run out of the mold more easily).
3. Pour the gel onto a transparent support, in the center, forming a square.
4. Wet the rubber seal only from underneath at the protrusion, then gently and evenly press it onto the support (use the tray to help).
5. Align the teeth so that the lower protrusions touch the support.
6. Slowly pour the cooled gel into the mold, starting from the lower edge and checking that the mold does not leak.
7. Run the tip through the wells; after the gel solidifies, this creates retaining feet, and remove bubbles from the gel.
8. Insert the comb and allow the gel to solidify.
9. Optional: place the solidified gel in the refrigerator for 10 min.

Preparing plugs for the gel

1. Pour a small amount of buffer onto the gel.
2. Prepare a burner, spatula, scalpel (the thinner, the better), and a beaker with buffer. Sterilize and cool the spatula and scalpel after each sample.
3. Cut the plugs so that they fit into the wells; finally, press them down to the bottom of the wells.
4. Concentrate the samples in the center; leave the wells at the edges empty to avoid possible bending of the lanes.
5. Pipette liquid LMPA gel and seal the plugs (2 drops into each well, including empty wells).
6. Transfer the gel together with the support into the apparatus.
7. Connect the apparatus, including the frame, and start program 9.

Gel staining and imaging

1. Pour EtBr (500 mL, 0.5 µL/mL in dH₂O) into an opaque container with a lid.
2. Remove the support with the gel from the apparatus and place it on paper towels.
3. Run the scalpel underneath the gel and smoothly cut off the feet (the gel will be released).
4. Use a square plate to lift the gel and transfer it to the opaque container with a lid. Place the plate next to the support and slide the gel onto it; handle the gel only from the sides.
5. Make sure the gel is floating and wash/agitate it for 1 h with gentle shaking.
6. Remove the feet from the support with a scalpel, rinse the support from the buffer, and flush the holes with the wash bottle.
7. After 1 h, use a funnel to pour the EtBr into the bottle (remove the stopper from the container). Pour carefully so that the gel does not block the outlet or become deformed.
8. Rinse the gel with dH₂O.
9. Add dH₂O to the container so that the gel is covered and floating.
10. Wash the gel for 1 h (destaining) with gentle shaking.
11. Transfer the gel in the container and photograph it using VisionWorks. Remove the gel using the plate, align it with a ruler, use the ruler as the focus point and zoom onto the gel; remove excess water with a paper towel so the gel does not float in the imaging chamber; adjust aperture and exposure parameters for a better image.
12. Dispose of the gel in the waste bin and clean everything used.

RNase treatment (band cleanup)

1. Mix 350 mL dH₂O + 700 µL 0.5 M EDTA (pH 8) + 17.5 µL RNase A (20 mg/mL).
2. Pour into the container with the gel.
3. Wash the gel several times and check that it is floating.
4. Place the gel in the container in the refrigerator for the weekend.

Chef DR III — gel preparation, plugs and program

1. Prepare 3 L 1× TAE (300 mL 10× TAE / 60 mL 50× TAE + dH₂O).
2. Pour 2.2 L into the apparatus and switch on the cooling with the pump.
3. Correctly and firmly screw the gel mold together.
4. Prepare 150 mL of 0.8% agarose gel (1.2 g LMP agarose), pour it into the mold, leaving a little for sealing.
5. Add the comb and allow the gel to solidify. After solidification, place it in the refrigerator for 15 min.
6. Remove the comb, pour in a little buffer, and place the plugs. Use a scalpel and spatula (ideally a burner for flaming); after each plug, wash in ethanol and cool in buffer.
7. Seal using the remaining liquid gel (2–3 drops with a large tip into each well, including wells without samples).
8. Remove the buffer, unscrew the mold, and transfer the gel into the apparatus.
9. Start the program and clean up afterwards.

Switching off the apparatus and staining

1. After the program has finished, discard the buffer (use it a maximum of twice, and even then according to judgment).
2. Run the pump with dH₂O only for cleaning, then discard the water.
3. Clean the tubing with dH₂O (there is a device for this in the sink).
4. Wipe the apparatus and clean up afterwards.
5. Transfer the gel in buffer/dH₂O to an opaque container with a lid and bring it to Silvia.
6. Pour off the liquid and add EtBr (500 mL, 0.5 µL/mL in dH₂O).
7. Make sure the gel is floating and wash/agitate for 1 h with gentle shaking. If this is not possible, simply leave it standing.
8. After 1 h, use a funnel to pour the EtBr into the bottle. Pour carefully so that the gel does not block the outlet or become deformed.
9. Rinse the gel with dH₂O.
10. Add dH₂O to the container so that the gel is covered and floating.
11. Wash the gel for 1 h (destaining) with gentle shaking. If this is not possible, simply leave it standing.
12. Photograph the gel; handle the gel carefully.
13. Dispose of the gel in the waste bin and clean everything used.
