Spot Test Calculator

How does the preparation work?

The spot test preparation starts from an overnight culture. For all three methods, the culture is refreshed to a target OD600 of 0.2 and grown for approximately 4–5 hours. The expected OD600 after growth is approximately 0.8–1.0.

Cells are then counted and the culture is normalized before preparing the spot dilution series.

For the two liquidator methods, the required cell concentration depends on the number of cells required per spot and the volume used for each spot. The normalized suspension has a final volume of 200 µL.

Spot test settings

Required concentration: 2,000 cells/µL

SAV – liquidator

Count cells manually using a Bürker chamber. Enter the average number of cells counted in one large square for each strain.

Cell concentration

cells/µL = average cells per large square × 250

The factor 250 corresponds to the volume of one large square (0.004 µL).

PrifUK – liquidator

Enter the concentration measured directly by the automated cell counter. The calculator determines the culture and water volumes required to prepare 200 µL at the required concentration.

PrifUK – multichannel pipette

This method represents the historical multichannel-pipette preparation. It uses a fixed highest concentration and a five-fold dilution series.

Highest concentration:

3,125 cells/spot in 3 µL

= 1,041.67 cells/µL

Dilution series

3,125 → 625 → 125 → 25 → 5 cells/spot

Each step represents a five-fold dilution.

Notes

  • All three methods use a final normalization volume of 200 µL.
  • For SAV and PrifUK liquidator methods, spot volume can be changed.
  • The required concentration is recalculated automatically from cells per spot and spot volume.
  • The multichannel method represents the historical preparation workflow.
